The case

The literacy crisis is not a vocabulary problem. It is a reading problem.

Word lists do not work. A word only means something inside a sentence that someone wrote. Superb starts with the book, and the vocabulary comes off it.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Is it free?

Yes, all of it. The books are public domain, the dictionary is open data, and everything runs in your browser. There is nothing to bill you for, and no paid tier behind any feature.

Which books do I get?

1,478 of them, carefully typeset from Standard Ebooks editions: fiction, poetry, drama, philosophy and memoir. Browse the whole library before you decide.

What actually happens when I tap a word?

A card opens on the page with the meaning that fits your sentence. It does not list every meaning the word has ever had. Keep the word and it comes back later in the practice you choose. Ordinary reading records nothing else.

Do I have to play the games?

No. Reading is the whole product on its own. The three games are there when you want them. They are the only part that teaches Superb anything about you.

Is there an app I can install?

Install it from your browser’s menu and it behaves like one. It gets its own icon and its own window, and a book you opened once reads with no connection. An Android build follows, then iOS.

How do I ask for something, or report something broken?

Open an issue on GitHub. Superb is built in the open, and every change starts there.
