## The gate: word encounters, deck vs passages

M2 DONE item 3, ADVISORY-007 §1: "the majority of word encounters... occur inside passages rather than in the deck." Counted at the word level — one passage carries several encounters — across every session in the run. `composed_for_support` is **expected to read zero**: it is unreachable under the mechanism this simulator drives (the composer still scores sourced-vs-composed with a `sourced_preference` multiplier, not the sourced/composed precedence ADR-015's third amendment describes; that precedence has not landed on `dev` as of this run). A reader seeing that column at zero is reading the mechanism correctly, not finding a bug — see `EncounterTally`'s own doc comment.

| configuration | deck | composed (gap) | composed (support) | sourced | total | gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ordinary | 126 | 255 | 0 | 10 | 391 | GREEN |
| deck-heavy (degraded) | 3600 | 676 | 0 | 124 | 4400 | RED |

**The falsifiability demonstration ADVISORY-007 §1 requires.** The ordinary configuration reads **GREEN**; the deliberately degraded, deck-heavy configuration (`battery::deck_heavy_config`: 20 real-word calibration draws a session instead of 1, no pseudoword noise diluting the count) reads **RED**. A gate that read green under both would be a tautology wearing a checkmark; it does not.

## Band coverage — directive 3, at ≥1 and ≥2

Read off the ordinary run's own due lists (not a separately sampled population): for every session with a nonempty due list, does *some* sourced excerpt in the live corpus carry at least one of those words in informative context, and does some excerpt carry at least two — existence in the corpus, independent of whether the composer actually picked it (the PR #34 verifier's own "joint statistic" question, `workspace/contract.md`). This is a different population from T3b's own headline figure (4.8% of the 20,001-word wordfreq teaching band, rank 5,000-25,000, covered by 2+ excerpts) — that population needs `data/pipeline/frequency.py`'s generated output, which nothing in this crate's CI regenerates, so it is not recomputed here. This number instead answers the question issue #36's trial actually needs: of the due lists this simulator's own engine produces against the real corpus, how many are servable from the sourced pool at all.

**176 of 176 sessions with a nonempty due list (100.0%) had at least one sourced excerpt covering a due word; 77 (43.8%) had an excerpt covering two or more.**

## Realized share and reachable ceiling — directive 4

**Realized sourced share at the shipped `sourced_preference` (2.4): 3.77%** of passage word-encounters (10 of 265 composed+sourced encounters).

**Reachable ceiling — the share at the most favourable `sourced_preference` this sweep tested ([2.4, 16.0, 128.0, 1024.0]): 3.38%, at multiplier 2.4.** Session-level pool share (sourced sessions / (sourced + composed) sessions), the same unit `CALIBRATION.md` reads for its own ceiling. Never a recommendation for `sourced_preference` — the prohibition that survives every ADR-015 amendment: `sourced_preference` is a calibrated output, never a hand-set number, and this sweep does not set it.

**Precision caveat, attached everywhere a number above derives from `Candidate.words`.** `workspace/contract.md` item 5b (TRIPWIRE): informative-context precision is measured at **≈44%, verifier-recomputed** against real selection — not the 56% still written in some places. Every sourced encounter, every band-coverage count, and every reachable-ceiling point above counts an excerpt as covering a word because `Candidate.words` claims it does; roughly a coin flip of those claims may not hold up as genuinely informative context. Nothing in this report, or in the simulator it comes from, uses `Candidate.words` for a *scheduling* decision — item 5b's own line — but every number that counts what the corpus claims inherits the claim's own unreliability, and is reported with that understanding rather than clean.
